Living with roommates is a great way to save money on rent, but it comes with one major challenge: **keeping track of who owes what**. Between rent, utilities, groceries, WiFi, and that Netflix subscription everyone uses, expenses can get messy fast. You might have used the best app to find roommates, but managing shared finances once you're living together is a whole different story.

The good news? There's an **app to split bills with roommates** for every situation. In this guide, we'll review the best roommate expense tracker apps in 2026 and help you find the perfect one for your shared apartment.

## What to Look for in a Roommate Expense App

Before diving into the apps, here's what makes a great roommate expense tracker:

- **Easy expense logging** — Adding expenses should take seconds, not minutes
- **Recurring expense support** — Rent and utilities repeat monthly; your app should handle this automatically
- **Multiple splitting options** — Not everything is split equally
- **Clear balance tracking** — See who owes whom at a glance
- **Offline access** — For when WiFi is down (ironic, right?)
- **Free or affordable** — You're splitting costs to save money, after all

## How to Track Monthly Apartment Expenses Effectively

Regardless of which app you choose, here's a system that works:

### 1. Set Up Your Shared Expenses Book

Create a dedicated space for all apartment expenses. In [Zedger](/), this would be a "Monthly Recurring Book" specifically for your apartment.

### 2. List All Recurring Expenses

Common shared apartment expenses include:

| Expense | Typical Frequency |
|---------|------------------|
| Rent | Monthly |
| Electricity | Monthly |
| Water | Monthly/Quarterly |
| WiFi/Internet | Monthly |
| Gas | Monthly |
| Maid/Cleaning | Monthly |
| Society Maintenance | Monthly/Quarterly |
| Netflix/OTT | Monthly |
| Grocery runs | Weekly |

### 3. Decide on Splitting Rules

Have a conversation early about how to split:

- **Rent**: By room size? Equal? Based on income?
- **Utilities**: Equal? By usage? By number of occupants?
- **Groceries**: Shared pool or individual purchases?
- **Subscriptions**: Equal split for shared ones

### 4. Log Expenses Immediately

The biggest mistake roommates make is saying "I'll add it later." You won't. Log expenses as soon as you make them.

### 5. Settle Monthly

Pick a date (1st or last day of month works well) to settle all balances. This prevents large amounts from accumulating and reduces awkwardness.

## Common Roommate Expense Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Unequal Room Sizes

**Problem**: One roommate has a larger room with attached bathroom.

**Solution**: Use percentage-based splitting in [Zedger App](/). For example:
- Large room: 40%
- Medium room: 35%
- Small room: 25%

### Scenario 2: Someone Moves Out Mid-Month

**Problem**: A roommate leaves on the 15th. How do you split that month's rent?

**Solution**: Use exact amount splitting. Calculate their pro-rated share and enter specific amounts for each person.

### Scenario 3: Shared Groceries vs Personal Items

**Problem**: Some groceries are shared (cooking oil, rice) while others are personal (specific snacks).

**Solution**: Only log shared purchases in the apartment book. Personal items stay personal.

### Scenario 4: Guest Stays Extended Period

**Problem**: Someone's partner stays over frequently, increasing utility usage.

**Solution**: Discuss and adjust utility splits accordingly, or have the roommate cover the difference.

## Why Recurring Expense Tracking Matters

Here's something most expense apps get wrong: **apartment expenses are predictable and recurring**.

You pay rent every month. Electricity comes monthly. WiFi is monthly. Yet most apps treat every expense as a one-time entry.

[Zedger's Monthly Recurring Book](/) was designed for exactly this pattern:

1. **Set it once**: Add rent, WiFi, and other fixed expenses once
2. **Auto-appears monthly**: These expenses show up automatically each month
3. **Update only when needed**: Just change the amount when bills vary (like electricity)
4. **Never forget**: No more "did anyone add the rent this month?"

This saves time and ensures nothing falls through the cracks.
