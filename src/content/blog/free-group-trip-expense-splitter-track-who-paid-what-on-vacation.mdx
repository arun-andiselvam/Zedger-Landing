## Logging Advance Payments Without Losing Track

Almost every trip starts spending money long before it starts. A rental deposit in March for a trip in July. Four flights booked on one person's card because they had the miles.

These advance payments are the single most commonly mishandled part of group trip accounting, for two opposite reasons. The fix is to log the advance the day you pay it, as its own entry, with its own date and the correct payer. An entry in the Trip Book, dated when the money actually left someone's account.

## Splitting Unequal Accommodations Fairly

Nobody has a good instinct for this one. It's the question that comes up in every trip planning chat, and it rarely gets a clean answer. How do you split a vacation rental when the rooms aren't equal?

The scenario is always some version of this. A six-bedroom house, $4,000 for the week. Two couples take the two en-suite rooms. Three friends split a bunk room. One person gets the converted office with a pull-out couch. Splitting $4,000 six ways charges the person on the pull-out the same as the couple with a private bathroom and a balcony, which nobody thinks is fair, including, usually, the couple.

**Percentage split.** Assign each room a share of the total based on what it's worth. The en-suites carry 22% each, the bunk room carries 8% per person, the office room carries 12%. The percentages have to add to 100, and the conversation about what each room is worth happens once, before the trip, when everyone is relaxed and generous rather than tired and counting.

**Unequal amounts.** Sometimes it's simpler to just assign dollar figures. This room is $900, that one is $600, the bunks are $400 each. Same idea, less math anxiety, and it works well when the rental listing already prices rooms separately.

**Exclude members entirely.**

Zedger supports all four splitting modes, equal, percentage, unequal, and exclude-member, on any entry, so you can use equal for the group dinner and percentage for the villa without leaving the same Trip Book. The practical advice we'd give: settle the accommodation split before anyone books, write it into the Book as the first entry, and never revisit it. Retroactive fairness arguments on day five have no winners.

## Getting Started With a Free Group Trip Expense Splitter

Here's the workflow that keeps trips clean, start to finish.

**Create the Trip Book early.** As soon as the trip is real, before flights, before the deposit. The Book should exist before the first dollar does.

**Add everyone immediately.** If someone joins the trip late or only for part of it, add them then. You can exclude them from expenses that predate their involvement.

**Settle the accommodation split first.** Agree on percentages or amounts for rooms before anyone books, and enter the rental as your first real expense with that split applied. Do it once, then leave it alone.

**Log advance payments as they happen.** Deposits, prepaid tours, flights booked on one card. Each one its own entry, dated accurately, credited to the right person.

**Log everything during the trip, not after.** Fifteen seconds at the table beats an hour of reconstruction on the flight home. Offline entries sync later, so poor connectivity is not an excuse to skip it.

**Use the right split for each cost.** Equal for the group dinner, unequal for the villa, exclude-member for the excursion three people skipped. Matching the split to reality is what stops small resentments from forming.

**Run Smart Settlements at the end.** Let it calculate the minimum set of transfers, share the result with the group, and execute the payments through whatever method you already use.

Seven steps, and the last one is usually the only one that takes any thought. That's the whole point of using a free group trip expense splitter instead of a notes app.
